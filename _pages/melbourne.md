<h3>About Melbourne</h3>
<hr/>

<p>
The 2026 IEEE CyberSciTech / DASC / PICom / CBDCom co-located conferences will be
held in <strong>Melbourne, Australia</strong> — the capital of the state of Victoria and
the country's second-largest city. Consistently ranked among the world's most liveable
cities, Melbourne is famous for its vibrant arts and music scene, hidden laneways and
street art, world-class coffee and dining, leafy gardens, and a rich sporting culture.
As one of the most multicultural cities in the world, it offers visitors an extraordinary
mix of experiences within an easy-to-navigate, walkable city centre.
</p>

<p>
The conference takes place from <strong>9–13 November 2026</strong>, during the Southern
Hemisphere's late spring — an ideal time to explore the city and its surrounding regions.
</p>

<br>
<h3>What to See &amp; Do</h3>
<hr/>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Laneways &amp; street art</strong> — Melbourne's famous cobbled laneways (Hosier Lane, Degraves Street) are filled with murals, hidden cafés, and boutique shops.</li>
  <li><strong>Federation Square &amp; the arts precinct</strong> — home to the Ian Potter Centre and steps from the National Gallery of Victoria (NGV), Australia's oldest and most-visited art museum.</li>
  <li><strong>Royal Botanic Gardens</strong> — 38 hectares of gardens along the Yarra River, in the heart of the city.</li>
  <li><strong>Queen Victoria Market</strong> — a historic open-air market with fresh produce, local crafts, and street food.</li>
  <li><strong>Melbourne Cricket Ground (MCG)</strong> — one of the world's great sporting stadiums and home of the National Sports Museum.</li>
  <li><strong>Yarra River &amp; Southbank</strong> — riverside promenades lined with restaurants, bars, and the Eureka Skydeck.</li>
  <li><strong>St Kilda</strong> — a seaside suburb known for its beach, foreshore, and a little penguin colony on the pier.</li>
  <li><strong>Day trips</strong> — the <a href="https://www.visitmelbourne.com/regions/great-ocean-road" target="_blank">Great Ocean Road</a>, the Yarra Valley wine region, and the penguins of <a href="https://www.visitmelbourne.com/regions/phillip-island" target="_blank">Phillip Island</a> are all within easy reach.</li>
</ul>

<br>
<h3>Getting Around</h3>
<hr/>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Trams</strong> — Melbourne runs the largest urban tram network in the world. A <a href="https://www.ptv.vic.gov.au/more/travelling-on-the-network/free-tram-zone/" target="_blank">Free Tram Zone</a> covers the central city, so travel within the CBD is free.</li>
  <li><strong>Public transport (myki)</strong> — trains, trams, and buses across greater Melbourne use the <a href="https://www.ptv.vic.gov.au/" target="_blank">myki</a> smartcard (contactless payment is also being rolled out).</li>
  <li><strong>From the airport</strong> — <a href="https://www.melbourneairport.com.au/" target="_blank">Melbourne Airport (MEL, Tullamarine)</a>, about 23 km north-west of the city, is the main international gateway. SkyBus, taxis, and rideshare connect the airport to the CBD.</li>
</ul>

<br>
<h3>Weather in November</h3>
<hr/>
<p>
November is late spring in Melbourne, with mild and pleasant conditions — daytime
temperatures typically range from around 11&deg;C to 24&deg;C. Melbourne is well known
for its changeable weather ("four seasons in one day"), so light layers and a compact
umbrella are recommended. During the conference the local time is
<strong>AEDT (UTC+11)</strong>.
</p>
