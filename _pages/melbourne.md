<h3>About Melbourne</h3>
<hr/>

<p>
The 2026 IEEE CyberSciTech / DASC / PICom / CBDCom co-located conferences will be
held in <strong>Melbourne, Australia</strong> — the capital of the state of Victoria and
the country's second-largest city. Consistently ranked among the world's most liveable
cities, Melbourne is famous for its vibrant arts and music scene, hidden laneways and
street art, world-class coffee and dining, leafy gardens, and a rich sporting culture.
As one of the most multicultural cities in the world, it offers visitors an extraordinary
mix of experiences within an easy-to-navigate, walkable city centre.
</p>

<p>
The conferences will take place at the
<strong><a href="https://www.rmit.edu.au/about/our-locations-and-facilities/locations/melbourne-city-campus" target="_blank">Melbourne City campus of RMIT University</a></strong>,
in the heart of Melbourne's central business district (CBD). The campus is surrounded by
public transport, restaurants, cafés, theatres, galleries, and shopping precincts, with
many of the city's major attractions within walking distance.
</p>

<p>
The conference takes place from <strong>9–13 November 2026</strong>, during the Southern
Hemisphere's late spring — an ideal time to explore the city and its surrounding regions,
and we warmly encourage participants to do so during their visit.
</p>

<br>
<h3>Experience Melbourne</h3>
<hr/>

<h4>Laneways, Street Art &amp; Café Culture</h4>
<p>
Melbourne is famous for its network of historic laneways and arcades filled with colourful
street art, independent cafés, boutique shops, and hidden bars. Popular spots include
Hosier Lane, Degraves Street, Centre Place, Hardware Lane, and the historic Block and
Royal Arcades. The city is also widely recognised for its coffee culture, from tiny
laneway espresso bars to contemporary specialty coffee venues.
</p>

<h4>Arts, Culture &amp; Museums</h4>
<p>
Major cultural attractions include the National Gallery of Victoria (NGV) — Australia's
oldest and most-visited art museum — ACMI (the Australian Centre for the Moving Image),
Melbourne Museum, State Library Victoria, Arts Centre Melbourne, and the UNESCO World
Heritage-listed Royal Exhibition Building. Throughout the year, the city hosts
exhibitions, theatre performances, concerts, and festivals.
</p>

<h4>Food &amp; Dining</h4>
<p>
Melbourne's multicultural population is reflected in its dining scene, with cuisines from
around the world — Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese, Thai, Indian, Malaysian, Middle
Eastern, Mediterranean, and modern Australian. Popular dining areas include Chinatown,
Lygon Street in Carlton, Victoria Street in Richmond, Fitzroy, and the many laneways of
the CBD.
</p>

<h4>Parks &amp; Gardens</h4>
<p>
Despite its urban character, Melbourne has many parks and gardens within walking distance
of the city centre, including the Royal Botanic Gardens — 38 hectares along the Yarra
River with more than 8,500 plant species — as well as Carlton Gardens, Fitzroy Gardens,
Flagstaff Gardens, and Birrarung Marr.
</p>

<h4>Sport &amp; Entertainment</h4>
<p>
Melbourne hosts major international sporting events throughout the year. The Melbourne
Cricket Ground (MCG) — one of the world's great stadiums and home of the Australian
Sports Museum — hosts Australian rules football, cricket, and concerts, while Melbourne
Park and Rod Laver Arena host the Australian Open tennis. Along the Yarra River, the
Southbank promenade is lined with restaurants, bars, and the Melbourne Skydeck.
</p>

<br>
<h3>Attractions Near the Conference Venue</h3>
<hr/>
<p>
The RMIT City campus is in central Melbourne, and many attractions can be reached on foot
or by a short tram ride:
</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>State Library Victoria</strong> — right next to the RMIT City campus, one of Australia's oldest cultural institutions, famous for its historic domed reading room, exhibitions, and public spaces.</li>
  <li><strong>Queen Victoria Market</strong> — a short walk away, this historic open-air market offers fresh produce, specialty food, souvenirs, and street food from around the world.</li>
  <li><strong>Carlton Gardens &amp; Melbourne Museum</strong> — landscaped heritage gardens surrounding the World Heritage-listed Royal Exhibition Building and Melbourne Museum, a short walk from campus.</li>
  <li><strong>Chinatown</strong> — along Little Bourke Street, within walking distance of RMIT, with a wide range of Chinese and other Asian restaurants, bakeries, and grocery stores.</li>
  <li><strong>Federation Square &amp; the Yarra River</strong> — opposite Flinders Street Station, home to cultural venues, restaurants, and public events; from here, walk along the river to Southbank, the NGV, and the Royal Botanic Gardens.</li>
</ul>

<br>
<h3>Explore Beyond the City Centre</h3>
<hr/>
<ul>
  <li><strong>St Kilda</strong> — a bayside suburb known for its beach, foreshore, and pier with a little penguin colony; easily reached from the city centre by tram.</li>
  <li><strong>Fitzroy &amp; Carlton</strong> — inner-city neighbourhoods known for independent shops, street art, and live music (Fitzroy), and the Italian dining precinct of Lygon Street (Carlton).</li>
  <li><strong><a href="https://www.visitmelbourne.com/regions/great-ocean-road" target="_blank">Great Ocean Road</a></strong> — one of Australia's most scenic coastal touring routes, with dramatic ocean views, rainforest, coastal towns, and the Twelve Apostles. A full-day or overnight trip is recommended.</li>
  <li><strong><a href="https://www.visitmelbourne.com/regions/phillip-island" target="_blank">Phillip Island</a></strong> — coastal scenery and wildlife experiences, most famously the Penguin Parade, where little penguins return to shore at sunset.</li>
  <li><strong>Yarra Valley &amp; Dandenong Ranges</strong> — less than an hour from Melbourne, offering wineries, local produce, forest scenery, walking trails, and picturesque towns.</li>
</ul>

<br>
<h3>Getting Around</h3>
<hr/>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Trams &amp; the Free Tram Zone</strong> — Melbourne runs the largest urban tram network in the world, and tram travel is free within the <a href="https://www.ptv.vic.gov.au/more/travelling-on-the-network/free-tram-zone/" target="_blank">Free Tram Zone</a>, which covers much of the CBD and Docklands. The RMIT City campus and many central attractions are located within or close to the zone.</li>
  <li><strong>Public transport (myki)</strong> — travel outside the Free Tram Zone on trains, trams, and buses requires a <a href="https://www.ptv.vic.gov.au/" target="_blank">myki</a> smartcard or another accepted payment method (contactless payment is being rolled out) — please check the latest information from Public Transport Victoria before travelling.</li>
  <li><strong>From the airport</strong> — <a href="https://www.melbourneairport.com.au/" target="_blank">Melbourne Airport (MEL, Tullamarine)</a>, about 23 km north-west of the city, is the main international gateway. <a href="https://www.skybus.com.au/" target="_blank">SkyBus</a> operates frequent services between the airport and Southern Cross Station in the CBD; taxis, rideshare services, and car hire are also available.</li>
</ul>

<br>
<h3>Weather in November</h3>
<hr/>
<p>
November is late spring in Melbourne, with mild and pleasant conditions — daytime maximum
temperatures around 22&deg;C and overnight minimums around 11&deg;C. Melbourne is well
known for its changeable weather ("four seasons in one day"), so we recommend bringing:
</p>
<ul>
  <li>A light jacket or sweater</li>
  <li>Comfortable walking shoes</li>
  <li>A compact umbrella or rain jacket</li>
  <li>Sun protection, including sunscreen and a hat — the Australian sun is strong, even in spring</li>
</ul>
<p>
During the conference the local time is <strong>AEDT (UTC+11)</strong>.
</p>
